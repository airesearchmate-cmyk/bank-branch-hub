The Bank Branch Location Dossier

A team based asynchronous capstone in foreign market site selection. Your team will gather data on five candidate countries, evaluate which is riskiest, and recommend one for a new bank branch. No meetings required.

I. The Brief

Your assignment

You are an analyst at a major international bank. Senior management is preparing to open a branch abroad and has narrowed the shortlist to four countries: Argentina, Finland, Mexico, and India. Your team will evaluate these four markets, plus one country of your own choosing, on fifteen factors covering demographics, wealth, financial conditions, governance, and economic freedom. Your job is two part. First, gather the actual data. Second, decide which country is riskiest to enter, and recommend which one the bank should choose.

How this works asynchronously Every part of this project is designed to be done on your own schedule. No meetings, no live calls, no synchronous polls. Your work syncs to your teammates automatically as you type. When you need to discuss something, you post in the team Canvas thread and teammates reply over the next day or two.

III. The Phases

Three phases, fully asynchronous

Each phase produces an artifact the next phase depends on. Phases are designed to overlap a little, so you can keep moving without waiting for everyone to finish.

Phase One

Spin and Set Up

Spin to receive your role and country. Read the brief, the role description, and the source library. Each member also nominates one country in the team Canvas thread to serve as the team's fifth country, with a paragraph of reasoning. Replies happen on each member's own schedule.

No meetings. Post and reply across several days.
DeliverableYour spun assignment is saved automatically. Country nomination posted in Canvas.
Phase Two

Gather the Data

Each member fills in the workbench data row for their assigned country. The team's fifth country is filled in collaboratively, anyone can edit those cells. Citations go in the source field for each cell. The Research Lead reviews teammates' work and posts questions in Canvas; teammates reply on their schedule.

Cells lock to their owner. Teammates see your data live but cannot overwrite it.
DeliverableWorkbench fully populated with data and sources. Graded individually by column ownership.
Phase Three

Decide and Submit

Each member writes a brief assessment in Canvas of which country is riskiest and why. Teammates reply over a few days. The team converges on a single recommendation. The Writing Lead drafts the memo using the Decision section below; the Quality Lead does a final check and submits to Canvas.

Risk discussion happens entirely in Canvas threads. The Decision section here syncs as the team types.
DeliverableFinal memo submitted to Canvas with the Decision section as its core.
IV. Roles

Who does what

Roles are randomly assigned by the spinner above. Every role is essential. If you are unsure what a role does, expand the card.

D
Data Lead
Owns the workbench's quality and completeness
You make sure every cell gets filled in, that data values look reasonable, and that the workbench is ready by the end of Phase Two. You are the one who notices when something is missing.
Phase 1
Confirm everyone has spun. Post in the Canvas thread by mid week with a list of who got what role and country.
Phase 2
Open the workbench daily. If a teammate's column is mostly empty by Wednesday, post a friendly reminder in Canvas. Audit suspicious or out of range numbers.
Phase 3
Verify the team's fifth country has data for all fifteen factors before the Decision section is finalized.
R
Research Lead
Reference checker and source auditor
The recommendation is only as good as its sources. You verify that every figure has a credible citation and that teammates are pulling from the right databases.
Phase 1
Read the source library. Post in the Canvas thread a brief note on which database is best for each of the fifteen factors.
Phase 2
Review each teammate's citations as they are entered in the workbench. If a source is missing or looks off, ask in the Canvas thread; the teammate replies on their schedule.
Phase 3
Confirm citations in the final memo follow APA format and link to a real, accessible source.
W
Writing Lead
Drafts the team's recommendation memo
An executive will read this memo. Your writing has to be clear, persuasive, and grounded in the data your team gathered.
Phase 1
Post a one paragraph plan in the Canvas thread for how the memo will be structured.
Phase 2
As workbench data comes in, take notes. Begin drafting country profile paragraphs.
Phase 3
Lead the Decision section below. Fill in the team consensus once teammates have all weighed in via Canvas. Compose the final memo and hand it to Quality Lead.
Q
Quality Lead
Final checkpoint before submission
You are the last set of eyes before submission. Your job is to make sure the deliverable is clean, complete, and on time.
Phase 1
Read the assignment rubric carefully. Post a checklist in the Canvas thread of what the final deliverable must include.
Phase 2
Track progress quietly. Confirm by end of phase that every column in the workbench has data and citations.
Phase 3
Review the memo from Writing Lead. Verify file format, naming, word count, and that all teammates are credited. Submit on Canvas before the deadline.
V. Sources

Where to find every statistic

These six databases cover all fifteen factors. Bookmark them. Citations in your memo should point back to one of these sources, not to a secondary blog or summary article.

01 · Macroeconomic CoreThe World Bank
Use for: Population, Population Growth, GNI, GNI per Capita, External Debt, Inflation, Lending Rate, Deposit Rate. Search by country and indicator name.
Open
02 · Cross CheckInternational Monetary Fund
Use for: verifying World Bank figures, sovereign debt, deposit rates. The IMF often has more recent quarterly figures.
Open
03 · Country SnapshotsCIA World Factbook
Use for: quick country profiles, Gini coefficient, income distribution. Each country has a single page covering all major indicators.
Open
04 · CorruptionTransparency International
Use for: Corruption Perceptions Index score and rank. Higher scores mean cleaner governance.
Open
05 · Political ClimateFreedom House
Use for: Political Rights, Civil Liberties, Freedom Status. Score range 1 to 7 where 1 is most free.
Open
06 · Economic FreedomHeritage Foundation
Use for: Index of Economic Freedom, Property Rights, Financial Freedom. Scale 0 to 100, higher is freer.
